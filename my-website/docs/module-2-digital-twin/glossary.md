# Glossary: Digital Twin Terminology

## A

**Avatar** - A digital representation of a human user in virtual environments, often used to simulate human presence in digital twin systems.

## D

**Digital Twin** - A virtual representation of a physical robot and its environment, used for safe testing, observation, and improvement before real-world deployment.

**Digital World** - A high-fidelity virtual environment that closely replicates real-world conditions for testing and training purposes.

## E

**Environment Modeling** - The process of creating digital representations of physical spaces, including objects, surfaces, and interactive elements.

## F

**Fidelity** - The accuracy and realism of a digital twin's representation of its physical counterpart.

**Flight Simulator** - An analogical reference system that demonstrates how digital twins provide safe practice environments, commonly used to train pilots before real flight.

## G

**Gravity Simulation** - The modeling of gravitational forces in virtual environments to ensure objects behave realistically.

## H

**High-Fidelity** - Having a high degree of accuracy and realism in representing physical characteristics and behaviors.

**Human-Robot Interaction** - The study and practice of how humans and robots communicate, collaborate, and coexist in shared environments.

## I

**IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit)** - A device that measures motion, orientation, and gravitational forces, including accelerometers, gyroscopes, and magnetometers.

**Interactive Simulation** - A simulation environment where users can manipulate variables and observe changes in real-time.

## L

**LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)** - A sensing method that uses laser light to measure distances to objects, creating detailed spatial maps.

## M

**Movement Mechanics** - The mathematical modeling of how objects translate (linear motion) and rotate (angular motion) in virtual environments.

**Modeling** - The process of creating mathematical or computational representations of real-world systems or objects.

## P

**Physics Simulation** - The mathematical modeling of physical laws such as gravity, friction, and collision to create realistic interactions in virtual environments.

**Point Cloud** - A collection of data points in 3D space, typically generated by LiDAR sensors to represent the shape and distance of objects.

## R

**Ray Casting** - A graphics and simulation technique that projects rays from a viewpoint to determine visibility, distance, or collision.

**Reality Gap** - The difference between simulated environments and real-world conditions that can affect the transfer of learning or behavior from simulation to reality.

**Robot Perception** - The ability of a robot to interpret and understand its environment through sensor data.

## S

**Sensor Fusion** - The combination of data from multiple sensors to achieve more accurate and reliable information than could be obtained from any single sensor.

**Simulation Environment** - A virtual space where digital twins operate and interact with their virtual surroundings.

**Spatial Mapping** - The process of creating 3D maps of physical environments using sensor data.

## T

**Transfer Learning** - The application of knowledge gained in one environment (such as simulation) to perform effectively in another environment (such as the real world).

## V

**Virtual Environment** - A computer-generated simulation of a real or imagined physical space.

**Virtual Practice Space** - A digital environment designed specifically for safe learning, testing, and experimentation without physical risk.

## W

**World Modeling** - The creation of comprehensive digital representations of physical environments, including geometry, physics, and interactive elements.

## Z

**Zero-risk Testing** - The ability to conduct experiments and trials without possibility of physical damage, injury, or negative real-world consequences.
